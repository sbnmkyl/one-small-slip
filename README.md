# One Small Slip: The Spatula Lost in Space

An animated, click-through picture book made for the **NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026**.

**Read it here:** https://sbnmkyl.github.io/one-small-slip/

## The story

On July 12, 2006, during the third spacewalk of NASA's STS-121 mission, astronaut Piers Sellers lost a spatula he was using to spread heat-shield repair putty. In this book the spatula tells its own story: from a kitchen drawer to astronaut training, launch, the moment it slips away, life among orbital debris, and its fiery return through the atmosphere.

A final "What really happened?" page separates the fiction from the verified facts.

## How to read

- Click the book, or press the right arrow / space bar, to show the next speech bubble. When a page's dialogue is done, the next click turns the page.
- Left arrow goes back. The dots under the book jump to any page.
- "Bubbles: auto / on click" switches between automatic speech bubbles and presenter mode.
- Works on phones too: swipe to turn pages.

## Why it matters

Objects in orbit travel at about 28,000 km/h, so even a tiny paint fleck can chip a spacecraft window. By turning one lost spatula into a story, the project helps children and the public understand why every object left in orbit matters, and why we need to keep space clean.

## Built with

A single self-contained HTML file: hand-drawn SVG illustrations, CSS animations and a little JavaScript. No build step, no dependencies (fonts load from Google Fonts).

Dedicated to the memory of Piers Sellers (1955–2016), climate scientist and astronaut.
