The Spatula Lost in Space

A slightly exaggerated story, inspired by a real spacewalk

STS-121 · July 2006

The family in the drawer

My family works in the kitchen. Mom flips pancakes, Uncle Ladle serves soup, and my cousin Whisk never stops beating eggs. I was the shiniest spatula in the drawer. Until one morning, a letter arrived from NASA.

Uncle LadleDid you hear? Our shiny spatula is going to space!
Cousin WhiskTo space?! No pancakes! No eggs! Not even a frying pan!
SpatulaNo pancakes, but plenty of stars. Maybe I’ll flip those too!
Mom (Wooden Spatula)Sweetie, wherever you go... hold on tight, okay? Don’t slip out of anyone’s hands!
1

Secret mission: space plasterer

My job was very serious. If the shuttle’s heat shield ever cracked, we would try to fix it with putty, right there in space. First we practiced in a pool, because floating in water feels a lot like floating in space. So yes: to get ready for space, I took swimming lessons.

AstronautThe job is simple, partner: put the putty on the crack and spread it smooth.
SpatulaLike spreading jam on toast?
AstronautExactly. Just one difference: this toast is moving at 28,000 kilometers an hour.
Spatula (thinking)Jam on toast has never been this exciting.
2

July 4, 2006: Liftoff!

The space shuttle Discovery rose into the sky. It was a holiday in America, and fireworks were popping everywhere. I was shaking inside the tool bag. From excitement or from the engines? I still don’t know.

SpatulaAre the fireworks for me?!
Striped AstronautIt’s Independence Day, spatula.
SpatulaIndependence? What a lovely word! Someday I’ll be independent too!
Little did the spatula know how soon that would be.
3

The spacewalk

July 12. The astronauts climbed out into the cargo bay, and I went with them. Below us the Earth was turning, like a giant blue pancake. The clouds were the powdered sugar on top.

Houston · radioDiscovery, this is Houston. You may begin the putty test.
AstronautCopy that. Spatula, you’re on!
SpatulaOops! Stripes, the putty splattered all over you. You look like a panda!
Striped AstronautThanks. It’s the latest fashion in space.
4

The moment

There’s no gravity in space. So if something slips out of your hand, it doesn’t fall. It just... leaves. In the one second my astronaut looked away, I left too.

SpatulaHey... HEY! I’m flying!
AstronautWhere’s the spatula? It was right here...
Houston · radioDiscovery, we see a small object drifting away from the cargo bay.
AstronautLooks like we have a new satellite.
Mom had said “hold on tight.” But in space, there was nothing to hold on to.
5

Goodbye, Discovery

The shuttle got smaller and smaller, until it was no bigger than a star. At first I was a little scared. Then I realized: for the first time in my life, I wasn’t stuck to anything. Not a pan, not putty, not a hand.

SpatulaDon’t worry! I’m fine! I’m just... drifting a little!
Striped Astronaut · radioTake care, little runaway!
SpatulaSay hi to everyone in the kitchen! If Mom asks, I’m in space for a while!
6

New neighbors

I wasn’t alone up there. Orbit was one giant lost-and-found: bolts, paint flecks, retired satellites... Nobody had an address, but everybody was very, very fast.

BoltWelcome, rookie! I escaped from a panel years ago.
SpatulaIs everyone this fast up here?
Bolt28,000 kilometers an hour, darling. Even the laziest one here is faster than a bullet.
Paint FleckDon’t call me tiny! At this speed I can chip a shuttle window!
Old SatelliteZzz... Keep it down. I’m retired.
7

I became famous

Down below, a small dot was blinking on Mission Control’s screen: me! I wasn’t just an ordinary spatula anymore. I was a space object with a name and an orbit. Mom would be so proud. Or very, very cross.

Mission ControlThe spatula’s path is calculated. No risk of hitting the shuttle or the station.
Coffee ControllerGreat. So... are there pancakes in the cafeteria today?
Spatula (from orbit)Relax, I’m a peaceful spatula. I don’t bump into anyone!
8

The view from above

The sun rose every 90 minutes. Morning came 16 times a day, and I never got breakfast once. From up there I noticed something: the air wrapped around Earth was a layer as thin as the oil in a pan. And this pan had no spare.

SpatulaMy astronaut was actually a climate scientist. Now I understand why he cared so much about Earth.
EarthTake good care of me, okay? I don’t have a spare planet.
SpatulaPromise! I’ve never seen such a beautiful pan... sorry, planet.
9

The biggest pan in the world

Slowly, over time, I sank lower and finally entered the atmosphere. I had spent my whole life working next to frying pans. Turns out the biggest pan of all was the sky. And at last, I got fried myself.

SpatulaHot! Hot! Somebody turn this burner down!
SpatulaBut I have to admit... I’ve never shone this bright.
KidMom, look! A shooting star!
MomQuick, make a wish!
SpatulaMy wish: May no tool ever get lost in space again!
10

What really happened?

July 4, 2006
The space shuttle Discovery launched on mission STS-121. Piers Sellers and Mike Fossum were on the crew.
July 12, 2006
On the mission’s third spacewalk, Sellers and Fossum tested a heat-shield repair putty called NOAX.
About 14 inches
The spatula used to spread the putty was about the size of a kitchen spatula. It floated away while Sellers wasn’t looking.
No danger
NASA tracked the spatula’s path; it posed no danger to the shuttle or the station. The repair tests were still a success.
28,000 km/h
That’s roughly how fast objects travel in orbit (about 17,500 mph). At that speed, even a tiny paint fleck can chip a window.
The real lesson
Astronauts tether their tools. And small objects in low orbit eventually fall back and burn up in the atmosphere.
SpatulaOkay, I exaggerated the story a little. Here are the facts!
SpatulaMost important lesson: in space, tie everything down. Look, now I’m tethered too!
11

The End

Look up at the sky tonight. Maybe you’ll spot the Spatula Constellation. And if you don’t, don’t worry: its closest relative is sitting in your kitchen drawer.

This book is dedicated to the memory of Piers Sellers (1955–2016), the climate scientist and astronaut who watched over Earth from space and worked to protect it.
