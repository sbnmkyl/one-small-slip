The Spatula Lost in Space

The problem

Orbit is a giant lost-and-found

  • Lost tools, bolts and paint flecks circle Earth at about 28,000 km/h.
  • At that speed, even a tiny paint fleck can chip a spacecraft window.
  • Space debris is a real risk for satellites and astronauts, yet most people never see it.

How do you make kids care about something they can’t see?

2 / 7

The true story

July 12, 2006: one small slip

  • Space shuttle Discovery, mission STS-121, third spacewalk.
  • Astronauts Piers Sellers and Mike Fossum were testing a heat-shield repair putty called NOAX.
  • The 14-inch spatula they used to spread it drifted out of the cargo bay.
  • NASA tracked it. It posed no danger to the shuttle or the space station.
3 / 7

Our solution

Let the spatula tell its own story

  • An 11-page animated picture book, narrated by the lost spatula.
  • Everyone talks in speech bubbles: astronauts, Mission Control, a bolt, a paint fleck, even Earth.
  • Kitchen jokes as metaphors: Earth as a blue pancake, the atmosphere as a thin layer of oil in a pan, reentry as the biggest frying pan.
  • Available in English and Turkish.
4 / 7

How it works

Built to be presented

  • Each click shows the next speech bubble, then turns the page with a paper-flip animation.
  • Presenter mode: bubbles wait for your click. Auto mode: they play on their own.
  • Works on laptops, projectors and phones.
  • One HTML file: hand-drawn SVG and CSS animation, no install, free on GitHub Pages.
5 / 7

The science inside

Real facts between the jokes

  • Why 28,000 km/h makes even tiny debris dangerous.
  • A 90-minute orbit means about 16 sunrises a day.
  • How thin Earth’s atmosphere really is, and why it needs protecting.
  • Why astronauts tether their tools, and how small objects burn up on reentry.

A final “What really happened?” page separates the story from the facts.
